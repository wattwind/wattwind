Auto mode

  • Starts in Auto, fan off, at every power-up: plug it in and ride.
  • Power, heart rate or both: follows whichever says you're working harder, or only one of them (Decide by, per rider); a missing one is covered by the other.
  • Your own numbers: the steps are set in % of your FTP and heart-rate range, so they fit a strong and a beginner rider alike, and read as training zones.
  • Up quickly, down slowly: smoothed power, so a short recovery doesn't switch the fan down.
  • No flapping: one level at a time, a gap between the up and down levels, a minimum time at each level.
  • Fan response: Calm, Normal or Quick, for how eagerly it follows you.
  • Stop pedaling: it holds a minute, then winds down to off; ride on and it carries on.
  • Sensor lost: runs on the other one; both lost: holds 2 minutes, then steps down.
  • Nudge − / +: up to two levels less or more for this ride; Auto still follows the workout.
  • Highest level: keep Auto at 1 or 2 for quiet evenings.
  • Idle off: off after 15 minutes (adjustable) without pedaling or sensor data.
  • Every threshold and time is a setting, with Reset to defaults.

Sensors

  • Strap and trainer at once over Bluetooth: any heart-rate strap or armband, any trainer or power meter with Cycling Power or FTMS.
  • Either one is enough: power only, or heart rate only (rollers, a basic trainer).
  • Finds your sensors by itself and remembers them, so it never grabs the strap of the rider next to you.
  • Find sensors: a list of what's nearby, pick one; Forget to change.
  • Reconnects by itself after a dropout.
  • Your training app stays connected to the same sensors (most allow two connections).
  • Passes your heart rate on (Share heart rate, off until ticked): for a strap that allows only one connection, the fan sends its heart rate on as a Bluetooth heart-rate sensor of its own, WattWind-XXXX HR, for one app or bike computer.
  • Strap taken off: noticed within seconds and shown as "(not worn)", even on straps that keep sending their last value; Auto then goes by power.
  • Sensor battery shown, with a warning at 15 % or less.

Riders

  • Up to 5 riders, each with their own FTP, heart-rate range, Decide by, fan response, step levels and strap.
  • Switches by strap: straps only send while worn, so the fan picks whoever's strap is on, within about 20 seconds.
  • Or by hand: a rider chosen in the list stays selected until you choose again.

Also an ordinary fan

  • Off · 1 · 2 · 3 from the page, no sensors needed; the level stays until you change it, also after a ride.
  • Off timer: Off in 30 min, 1 h or 2 h, then it stays off.
  • Forgotten? After 8 hours in Manual (up to 24, or never) it goes back to Auto, which turns it off when nobody rides.
  • Back to Auto mid-ride continues from the current level, no sudden drop.

Linked fans

  • Several fans, one ride: any number of WattWinds follow one leader over your home Wi-Fi.
  • Each at its own level: same as the leader, one higher, one or two lower, or its own Auto settings from the leader's heart rate and power.
  • One remote: the leader's buttons and off timer move every follower that follows its level.
  • Sensors stay free: followers don't use Bluetooth at all.
  • Leader gone: followers hold a minute, then step down to off.
  • Pairing in a minute: Pair a new fan on the leader, Follow on the other; Unpair all followers and Pair again when plans change.
  • Encrypted and signed: other devices on your network can't read or fake the messages.

The phone page

  • Served by the fan itself: no app, no account, no internet; light and dark.
  • Works on older phones too: plain web code, made for iPhones from about iOS 13 and Android phones with Chrome from about 2019, and current browsers on a computer.
  • On the home screen it opens like an app.
  • Readable on the handlebars: a big level, the mode, what happens next, live watts, rpm and bpm, the demand and what decided it, sensor dots.
  • The fan's name in the header, so with several fans you know which one you're on.
  • Warnings when something needs you: a low sensor battery, a hot controller, damaged firmware files, a safe start.
  • Never loses your input: unsaved changes are marked, with Save and Undo, and it asks before you leave; a wrong value is marked at its field with what to change.
  • Lost contact is shown: values grey out, and after a restart the page finds the fan again by itself.
  • Built-in manual behind the ?, the same one as on this site.

Wi-Fi

  • At http://wattwind.local on your home network, with the IP shown for Android; give each fan its own name.
  • Its own network WattWind-XXXX when there's no home network, and the page opens by itself when you join it.
  • Find networks lists what the fan can use, strongest first.
  • Test Wi-Fi before switching: shows the address your router gives it, reports a wrong password at once, saves nothing.
  • Router gone (or slow after a power cut): its own network after 2 minutes, then back home by itself.
  • Fan control never depends on Wi-Fi.

Security and privacy

  • Your own password and PIN at the first setup: no fan keeps the published default.
  • Settings PIN for settings, Wi-Fi, updates, logbook and backups (the fan buttons too, if you like); each device asks once; wrong guesses are slowed down.
  • Only official firmware: updates must carry the release's digital signature.
  • Websites can't reach it through your browser: it answers only to its own name and address, and takes commands only from its own page.
  • Your data stays home: no cloud, no account; the logbook keeps no heart rate or power; the only thing it asks the internet is the time.
  • Forgot the PIN? Plug it in and out three times, then a fourth time and leave it: new PIN, everything else kept.
  • Erase everything before you give it away.
  • Compared with ETSI EN 303 645, the consumer smart-device baseline, as a guide.

Maintenance and diagnostics

  • Firmware update over Wi-Fi from the page: signature and every file checked first; if anything fails, nothing changes. The box stays closed.
  • Check for updates: one tap asks GitHub from your phone (not the fan) whether there's a newer version; or get an email from GitHub when one comes out.
  • Backup and restore of all settings, on this fan or another; passwords only if you choose.
  • Logbook: every start and why, every level change with its reason, Wi-Fi and sensor events, errors; about 4½ to 9 days of a busy fan.
  • Diagnostics file to download and send when asking for help.
  • Detailed recording: every second of a ride for 24 h, for a problem that's hard to catch.
  • A line at the bottom of Settings: version, running time, why it last restarted, Wi-Fi signal, chip temperature, sensor batteries.
  • Firmware file check at every start.
  • Restart from the page.

Built to fail safe

  • Relays off first at every start, before anything else runs.
  • Both wires disconnected when the fan is off; one stuck relay alone can't keep it running.
  • Never two speeds at once: break-before-make, at most one change per 2 seconds.
  • Hardware watchdog: a program that stops responding restarts with the fan off.
  • Every part restarts on its own after an error; an error can't leave a relay stuck.
  • Settings checked at every start; a bad value goes back to its default.
  • Safe start: after three error restarts in a row it starts with default ride settings, so the page stays reachable for a fix.

The hardware

  • Your own 3-speed fan, up to 100 W (185 W with the bigger fuse); 230 V, 120 V untested.
  • One board, 16 through-hole parts, 65 × 82 mm, ordered online from one ZIP file.
  • Mains kept apart: 8 mm between mains and low voltage, a fuse, the wiring diagram printed on the back.
  • Tested without mains first: the relays click through every level on USB power.
  • A printed box made by a script: change a number and it rebuilds and checks its own fit; ready-made print files too.
  • Closed and labelled: lid screws from underneath, an engraved rating plate, no USB opening unless you want one.
  • Straps to the fan's stand with two cable ties; one cable to the fan.
  • Or your own box, with a list of what it needs.
  • About €70–80 in parts.

Open

  • All of it published: firmware (MIT), board and box (CERN-OHL-P), site and docs (CC BY).
  • Change anything: plain MicroPython, a KiCad board, a Fusion script; your own firmware with your own signing key.
  • Tested on the computer: 400+ tests, including whole simulated rides and a real ride replayed second by second.
  • Measured on a real ride: the same power, heart rate and cadence as a bike computer, under a second later.
